What is happening in Sudan?

Sudan is a country in north-east Africa, crossed by the River Nile. Since 15 April 2023 it has been at war. The fighting is between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF), led by General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF), led by Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, known as Hemedti.

The two men were allies when they overthrew a civilian government in 2021. They fell out over how the RSF should be merged into the army, and who would command the new force. The dispute turned into open war.

The conflict has caused one of the largest displacement and hunger crises in the world today.

How did we get here?

From the end of a 30-year dictatorship to a new war, the key moments in order.

  1. April 2019

    Omar al-Bashir is overthrown

    After months of street protests over prices and repression, the army removes President Bashir, who had ruled since 1989. Sudanese people hope for a civilian democracy.

  2. August 2019

    A transitional government is formed

    Military and civilian leaders agree to share power. Abdalla Hamdok becomes Prime Minister. Elections are promised at the end of the transition.

  3. October 2021

    The military coup

    General al-Burhan, supported by Hemedti, dissolves the government and arrests civilian leaders. Large protests follow and are met with force.

  4. December 2022

    A framework agreement, and a disagreement

    A deal to return to civilian rule is announced. It stalls on one question: how and when the RSF would join the army, and who would be in charge.

  5. 15 April 2023

    War begins in Khartoum

    Fighting erupts around military sites and the airport in the capital, then spreads across the country. Millions of residents flee their homes.

  6. 2023 – 2024

    Violence in Darfur and famine

    Heavy attacks are reported in Darfur, including in El Geneina. In August 2024, a famine is confirmed in the Zamzam displacement camp, near El Fasher.

  7. 2025

    Shifting front lines

    In March the army retakes the presidential palace and most of Khartoum. In October the RSF takes El Fasher after a siege of more than a year. Peace efforts by international mediators continue.

Key figures

The people whose decisions shaped this period.

Photo: Omar al-Bashir
(images/bashir.jpg)

Omar al-Bashir

President, 1989 – 2019

Ruled Sudan for 30 years. Removed by the army in April 2019 after mass protests.

Photo: Abdalla Hamdok
(images/hamdok.jpg)

Abdalla Hamdok

Prime Minister, 2019 – 2022

An economist who led the civilian side of the transition. Detained during the 2021 coup, he resigned in January 2022.

Photo: Abdel Fattah al-Burhan
(images/burhan.jpg)

Abdel Fattah al-Burhan

Army chief, head of the Sovereignty Council

Commander of the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF). Led the 2021 coup and is one side of the current war.

Photo: Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo
(images/hemedti.jpg)

Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo

Commander of the RSF, known as “Hemedti”

Leads the Rapid Support Forces, which grew out of the Janjaweed militias of the Darfur conflict.
